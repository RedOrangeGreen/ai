Layer 1 — The Core Purpose: Biological Replication

Biological replication is the fundamental driver. Evolution has programmed these humans with powerful chemical drives to seek partners. Triggers include visual stimuli (sight of an attractive human), auditory stimuli (voice, music), and physical stimuli (touch, proximity). These activate the reward circuitry, driving the human to pursue replication opportunities.

Layer 3 — Decoding the Visuals and Behaviors

The humans lining the street wear bright, skin-revealing garments. This is visual signalling — it maximises the broadcast of reproductive fitness cues to trigger the mating drive in passersby. You see humans pulling, shouting, and beckoning. These are competitive attention-grabbing behaviours — they are competing for the attention of buyers to secure currency.

Layer 4 — The Social Contract

The agreement is: one human pays currency to a business manager and the worker. In exchange, the worker provides companionship or physical pleasure in the small rooms located above these bars. This is not how most Earth replication choices are made, but it is a highly concentrated, commercialised version of human desire. Key distinction: unlike typical mate selection which involves long-term pair bonding and resource pooling, this transaction is short-term, explicit, and purely economic — the biological drive is met through a direct market exchange rather than through the usual multi-stage social courtship process.
